# External research challenge

Evidence retrieved: **2026-10-26**. This is a targeted review of primary research and an identified practitioner study, not a systematic literature review or a meta-analysis. Repository orientation read: `AGENTS.md`, `QUANT_NORTH_STAR.md`, and `governance/CURRENT_PROJECT_STATE_2026-10-20.md`. Review workspace at inspection: `review/independent-architecture-challenger-20261026`, HEAD `f503b5f070bda6a61824d139ba8bb7b026892ebe`.

**Conclusion:** external evidence supports explicit experimental identity, source timing, complete trial accounting, protected confirmation, scientific memory, and economic evaluation. It does **not** establish that Quant needs a new discovery subsystem, that mechanism-first search dominates bounded statistical search, or that one of Research OS, F, V3, or a proposed Advanced Research architecture will discover more net alpha. Those are project-specific empirical questions.

External sources below describe desirable properties and known failure modes. They provide no evidence that Quant contains, lacks, integrates, deploys, or uses any capability. An inference is labeled as such. Numerical results describe the cited sample; they are neither current trading opportunities nor estimates for Quant.

## Discovery and source diversity

| Research approach | Primary evidence and its limits | Property to test in Quant; architectural inference |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism-first vs statistical search | [DellaVigna and Pollet, *Investor Inattention and Friday Earnings Announcements* (2009)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01447.x) tests attention through announcement timing and differential response. [Gu, Kelly and Xiu, *Empirical Asset Pricing via Machine Learning* (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhaa009) reports predictive and portfolio improvements from nonlinear interactions of existing signals. These are different designs, not a randomized comparison of discovery architectures. | Record a mechanism or explicit anomaly claim, alternatives, falsifiers, and a discovery mode. Mechanism plausibility is useful prior information, not proof of predictability; successful prediction does not identify a causal mechanism. Permit both modes under a shared validation contract. No evidence here supports a mechanism-only veto or unrestricted statistical mining. |
| Replication and transfer | [Chen and Zimmermann, *Open Source Cross-Sectional Asset Pricing* (2021 working paper; published 2022)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/open-source-cross-sectional-asset-pricing.htm) reproduces nearly all clearly significant original predictors in its construction. [Hou, Xue and Zhang, *Replicating Anomalies*, NBER working paper (2017)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394) finds many anomalies insignificant after mitigating microcaps and using value weights. Different samples, definitions and construction choices matter. | Distinguish exact reproduction, robustness, transfer, and discovery. A transfer to a new asset changes transport assumptions and requires its own timing, feasibility and independent evidence. A different ticker is not automatically a different mechanism. Neither replication success nor failure warrants a universal acceptance rate. |
| Event, rule and constraint driven ideas | [Shleifer, *Do Demand Curves for Stocks Slope Down?* (1986)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1986.tb04518.x) studies index additions and index-fund buying. [Froot and Dabora, *How Are Stock Prices Affected by the Location of Trade?* (1998 working paper; published 1999)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w6572) studies twin firms with shared cashflows but different trading habitats. These historical examples demonstrate researchable restrictions, not current executable arbitrage. | Keep contract/rule/cashflow hypotheses as a legitimate question family. Encode eligibility, effective times, settlement, borrow, tax and execution assumptions before looking at returns. A rule establishing a payoff relationship does not establish feasible convergence or positive net P&L. |
| Alternative data | [Froot, Kang, Ozik and Sadka, *What Do Measures of Real-time Corporate Sales Tell Us about Earnings Surprises and Post-Announcement Returns?* (2016 working paper; published 2017)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22366) builds sales proxies from several sources, including mobile-device observations, and distinguishes within-quarter from post-quarter information. | A source contract must state observable time, delivery latency, vintages/revisions, coverage and admissible use. Availability, acquisition cost and sustainable access are separate from signal quality. This evidence does not imply Quant can obtain the authors' data or that another alternative-data generator is warranted. |
| Document and fundamental research | [Loughran and McDonald, *When Is a Liability Not a Liability? Textual Analysis, Dictionaries, and 10-Ks* (2011)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01625.x) shows that generic negative-word dictionaries misclassify common financial terms. [Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski, *Decoding Inside Information* (2010 working paper; published 2012)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16454) distinguishes routine from opportunistic insider transactions. | Preserve document fragments and extraction evidence; verify financial semantics and public availability separately. A new document does not imply a new hypothesis. An economically motivated distinction should become an ex ante feature definition and falsifiable test, not a retrospective narrative. The papers do not validate Quant's SEC adapters or documentary candidates. |
| Microstructure and flows | [Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov, *The Price Impact of Order Book Events* (2014)](https://academic.oup.com/jfec/article-abstract/12/1/47/816163?login=true) relates contemporaneous order-flow imbalance and price changes, conditional on depth. [Lou, *A Flow-Based Explanation for Return Predictability* (2012)](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/25/12/3457/1594242) tests flow-induced trading, temporary impact and subsequent reversal. | Separate contemporaneous explanation from tradable forecasting. Require event ordering, latency, depth, impact and counterparty/flow assumptions appropriate to the horizon. Reuse a common experiment/identity seam; a source-specific model can differ without owning another research lifecycle. |
| Cross-sectional signals | Gu/Kelly/Xiu and the two replication studies above show both useful predictive structure and material sensitivity to evaluation conventions. [McLean and Pontiff, *Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?* (2016)](https://www.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.12365) studies 97 predictors and reports lower returns out of sample and after publication. | Register universe, breakpoints, weighting, delistings, factors, publication exposure and vintage. Benchmark against simple known predictors. Label economic identity and shared exposure; a parameter change or nonlinear combination is not automatically independent discovery. Current usefulness requires fresh admissible evidence. |

## Bounded search, validation and learning

| Issue | Evidence | Minimum defensible operational implication (inference) |
|---|---|---|
| Bounded empirical search and multiple testing | [Harvey, Liu and Zhu, *… and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns*](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592) models the multiplicity of factor research. [Sullivan, Timmermann and White, *Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rule Performance, and the Bootstrap* (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00163) evaluates the search universe rather than only a winning rule. | A cap bounds one cohort's search; reaching it does not prove scientific search-space exhaustion. Preserve tested variants and the family universe across cohort renewal. Select an appropriate multiplicity method and assumptions; the often cited factor t-statistic hurdle is not a universal threshold for every event study or data design. |
| Researcher degrees of freedom | [Novy-Marx, *Backtesting Strategies Based on Multiple Signals* (2015)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21329) shows severe selection bias from combining and signing signals. [Simmons, Nelson and Simonsohn, *False-Positive Psychology* (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611417632) demonstrates how undisclosed analysis flexibility increases false positives in a different field. | Log signs, windows, exclusions, outcomes, transformations and stopping decisions, including failed combinations. Duplicate suppression alone does not account for all trials; a cosmetic proposal rename must not reset exposure. These sources support disclosure and accounting, not weakening controls to meet a throughput target. |
| Preregistration and OOS | [Nosek et al., *The Preregistration Revolution* (2018)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29531091/) separates generating hypotheses from testing predictions. McLean/Pontiff documents why in-sample performance is an unreliable expectation. | Freeze estimand, data/vintage, analysis, decision rules and termination before protected outcomes. Record deviations as new exploratory lineage. Retrospective preregistration cannot make already seen outcomes unseen. OOS is only as independent as the information used to select the protocol. |
| Adaptive research policy | [Dwork et al., *The Reusable Holdout: Preserving Validity in Adaptive Data Analysis* (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26250683/) addresses adaptive reuse of data. [Javanmard and Montanari, *Online Rules for Control of False Discovery Rate and False Discovery Exceedance*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09000) proves controls under stated p-value assumptions. Neither theorem automatically covers dependent financial trials or arbitrary agent behavior. | Policy may learn from completed exploratory work and source productivity, but must version its information set, budget, dependence assumptions and next cohort before confirmation. Fresh cohort IDs are not fresh statistical evidence. Do not mechanically install a reusable holdout or alpha-investing rule without showing its assumptions fit. |
| Scientific memory and negative results | [Franco, Malhotra and Simonovits, *Publication Bias in the Social Sciences: Unlocking the File Drawer* (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25170047/) uses a known population of 221 studies and finds strong results more likely to be written up and published. This is evidence about publication selection, not Quant. | Preserve negative results, nulls, duplicate admissions, data blocks and nonexecution with denominators. Learn source productivity conditional on accessible/testable opportunities, not only positives. A data block is not a negative scientific result; a failed replication must retain its design differences. |
| Scientific vs economic rejection | [Bajgrowicz and Scaillet, *Technical Trading Revisited* (2012)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X1200116X) combines false-discovery, persistence and cost analysis for a specific technical-rule universe, finding economically unhelpful results there. | Keep evidence of statistical predictability separate from after-cost implementability and ex ante selection. A sound research rejection can be a productive outcome. This paper does not disprove every statistical strategy, asset, frequency or mechanism. |
| Portfolio and economic evaluation | [Frazzini, Israel and Moskowitz, *Trading Costs* (2018), practitioner working paper](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/working-paper/trading-costs) uses institutional executions and shows costs depend on instrument/trade/size/time. [DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal, *Optimal Versus Naive Diversification* (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhm075) illustrates how estimation error can overwhelm portfolio optimization gains. | Require exposure/cashflow identity, uncertainty, implementability, costs, capacity, benchmark and incremental portfolio contribution. Do not invent correlations without admissible returns. A shadow/evaluation chain can test contracts, accounting and feedback without live trading; it cannot establish realized live execution quality. Practitioner cost estimates are context-specific and not Quant's costs. |

## LLM ideation and autonomous-agent challenge

[Si, Yang and Hashimoto, *Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas?* (ICLR 2025)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ea94957d81b1c1caf87ef5319fa6b467-Abstract-Conference.html) compares human and model ideas using more than 100 NLP researchers. Model ideas received higher novelty judgments but slightly weaker feasibility judgments; the authors report diversity and self-evaluation failures. The measured outcome is an expert assessment in NLP, not independent quant discoveries or after-cost returns. **Inference:** use an LLM as a proposal source, measure duplicates and testability, and keep producer/challenger functions distinct. Counting fluent proposals is an inadequate research metric.

[Yamada et al., *The AI Scientist-v2* (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08066) describes hypothesis generation, managed experiments, analysis and manuscript production. Three manuscripts were submitted to an ICLR workshop; one passed the stated acceptance threshold. [Lu et al., *Towards End-to-End Automation of AI Research* (Nature, 25 March 2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10265-5) evaluates template-based and template-free ML research and reports workshop review success, explicitly at a lower bar than a full conference. These are related project reports, **not two independent replications**. Neither demonstrates robust autonomous economic discovery in changing markets. **Inference:** durable experiment execution and continuation are plausible capabilities, but the existence of an external agent architecture is no reason to replace an existing lifecycle or add a second controller.

The 2026 paper is included to avoid treating 2024/2025 agent results as the current evidence ceiling. This targeted retrieval does not establish that it is the latest or strongest result across every scientific domain. Public acceptance, model review scores and paper production remain proxies distinct from reproducible discovery and economic usefulness.

## What this evidence can and cannot decide

It supports testing whether the actual machine can preserve identity and outcomes while moving a source-grounded question through admission, frozen experiment, independent adjudication, economic evaluation and durable learning. It also supports reporting all attempts and distinguishing exploration from confirmation across generations.

It cannot rank RI versus Research OS versus V3 versus F; prove that frontier renewal is absent; diagnose UST's missing execution; remove SEC access restrictions; resolve Owner authority; prove that validation standards are systematically too restrictive; or prove a need for Advanced Research. Those require repository code, state, runtime evidence and controlled project comparisons.

A defensible architecture comparison would preregister equal source/compute budgets and source-access conditions, compare existing and proposed policies on **distinct admissible testable hypotheses per budget**, duplicate/exposure rates, positive-continuation completeness, calibrated scientific outcomes and after-cost out-of-sample incremental value. Report blocked-source time and unresolved follow-ups separately. Do not select a policy merely because it produces more ideas or one striking backtest. Small campaigns may remain underpowered to rank research quality; UNKNOWN is then the correct result.
