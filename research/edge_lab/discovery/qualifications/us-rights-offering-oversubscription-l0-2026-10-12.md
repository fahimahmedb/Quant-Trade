## Mechanism screened

MECHANISM = Some rights offerings grant record-date shareholders who fully exercise their basic subscription rights an over-subscription privilege for shares left unsubscribed by others, often at the same subscription price.
WHO_PAYS = Economically, non-participating/diluted shareholders and the issuer's capital-raising terms.
WHY_EDGE_MIGHT_EXIST = Allocation can be operationally cumbersome and over-subscription may be prorated, creating event-specific uncertainty.

## Primary-document observations

Fresh SEC primary filings reviewed at L0 show that the over-subscription privilege is generally tied to record-date ownership and full exercise of basic rights, and excess shares are commonly allocated pro rata when demand exceeds supply. Some offerings make the basic rights transferable, but explicitly deny over-subscription rights to purchasers who were not record-date shareholders. Other filings similarly condition over-subscription on retaining/fully exercising rights originally issued to the record-date holder.

Examples reviewed include 2026 SEC prospectus materials where: (a) non-transferable rights were issued to record-date holders with an over-subscription privilege after full basic exercise; (b) transferable-right purchasers who were not record-date holders were explicitly ineligible for over-subscription; and (c) over-subscription allocations were pro rata based on basic subscriptions. No issuer market price, post-offer return, P&L, hit rate, or realized allocation economics was read or computed.

## Why PARK_ECONOMICS at L0

The mechanism is real, but the currently observed structure does not show a clean small-actor asymmetry comparable to odd-lot priority or fractional round-up:

- eligibility is primarily a record-date entitlement rather than a post-public-announcement discretionary misallocation;
- transferable-right pricing can capitalize the basic subscription value;
- over-subscription is commonly proportional to basic participation rather than preferential to small holders;
- buying shares before a known record date to manufacture eligibility introduces ordinary directional exposure and does not identify a distinct payer or persistence mechanism;
- any apparent discount must be compared against the value/cost of the rights and dilution, so a headline subscription-price discount is not itself an edge.

Therefore no prospective L1 is opened now. A future revisit requires a causally stronger variant documented before outcome access, such as an explicit small-holder allocation priority, non-pro-rata residual allocation, or another rule that creates a discrete entitlement not scalable in proportion to capital. Do not search historical offerings for a winning example.

## Exposure note

This L0 used documentary SEC searches only. Rights-offering terms and several 2026 examples were exposed, so those issuers are not pristine future confirmation cases for this exact hypothesis. No market-price payload was intentionally accessed.
