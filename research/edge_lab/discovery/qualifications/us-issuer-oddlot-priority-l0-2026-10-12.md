## Decision and causal distinction

Some partial **issuer** tenders allow eligible beneficial owners of fewer than
100 shares to receive priority over prorated larger positions. The issuer pays
from its repurchase budget. The hypothesized rent is preferential allocation,
not directional equity forecasting. Small absolute capacity, administrative
costs and offer-specific conditions could prevent institutions from scaling it;
these are persistence hypotheses, not demonstrated excess returns. Capacity is
per eligible beneficial owner, not multiplied by brokerage accounts.

This differs from insider-information Form 4, ETF B2/B4, crypto carry,
prediction-market negative risk and the exact rejected electricity expression.
The parked Form-4 census is neither used nor recrawled. Legal priority can exist
while every purchasable position has negative expected profit.

   The SEC describes priority for sub-100-share owners under Rule 13e-4 and

   Question 101.04 distinguishes precommencement SC TO-C from commenced
   SC TO-I/SC TO-T. This supports a document-based eligibility screen; it does
